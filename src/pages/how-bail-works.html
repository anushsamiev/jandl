The process

How bail works, in plain English.

If this is your first time dealing with an arrest, here is everything that happens between the phone call and the release, and what it will cost.

What bail is

When someone is arrested, a judge or a bail schedule sets a dollar amount the court holds as a promise that the person will come back for every hearing. Pay it in full and the court returns it at the end of the case. Most families can’t put up thousands of dollars on a Tuesday night, which is where a bail bond comes in.

A bail bond is a guarantee we make to the court for the full amount. In exchange, you pay us a premium, a percentage of the bond, and the person is released while the case moves through court.

What you pay

Louisiana sets the premium by statute: 12% of the bond amount, or $120, whichever is greater. That number is the same at every licensed bail office in the state. The premium is earned when the bond is posted and is not refunded, even if the charges are dropped. Courts may add a small fee of their own on top.

On larger bonds we may ask for collateral: a vehicle title, property, or a cash deposit that is returned when the case closes and the defendant has made every appearance. Many bonds need no collateral at all, just a co-signer with a steady address and income.

What you’ll need when you call

  • The defendant’s full legal name and date of birthSo we can find them in the jail’s system and confirm the charges and bond amount.
  • Where they’re being heldCaddo Correctional Center, Shreveport City Jail, Bossier, or elsewhere. If you don’t know, we can find out.
  • A co-signer with a photo IDUsually a family member or friend over 18 who agrees to make sure the defendant shows up to court.
  • A way to pay the premiumCash, debit, or credit card. Ask about a payment plan if you need one.

How long it takes

We can usually have paperwork done and the bond delivered within an hour of your call. Release itself depends on the jail. Caddo Correctional Center processes releases around the clock, but booking volume, shift changes, and holds from other agencies can add hours. We’ll give you an honest estimate once we’ve talked to the jail, and we stay in touch until they’re out.

After release

The defendant must attend every court date until the case is finished. We’ll give you the first date in writing and remind you before each one. If a date is missed, the court can revoke the bond and issue a warrant, so call us right away if there’s a conflict. We’d rather help you reschedule than see a warrant issued.

Common situations

Someone has a warrant but hasn’t been arrested yet

We can arrange the bond in advance so they can turn themselves in and be released the same day, often without spending a night in jail. See warrant walk-throughs.

Bond hasn’t been set yet

In Caddo Parish, many charges have a preset bond schedule, so a bond is often available within hours of booking. For others, a judge sets bail at a first appearance, usually within 72 hours. Call us either way and we’ll track it.

The bond is more than the family can afford

Talk to us before deciding it’s out of reach. Between a payment plan, collateral, and multiple co-signers, most bonds can be worked out.
